Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in North Richland Hills, TX

Home / Blog / Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in North Richland Hills, TX

Hardwood floors are one of the most valuable features in any home, and they are also among the most vulnerable to water damage. Boards that get wet can stain, buckle, crown, or cup, and mold may grow in the subfloor underneath. For North Richland Hills, TX homeowners with hardwood floors, it is important to understand how to respond to water exposure and when professional restoration can save your floors versus when replacement is necessary.

How Water Damages Hardwood

Because wood is hygroscopic, it takes in moisture and gives it back as the conditions around it change. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. The earliest sign you can see of a moisture imbalance is cupping, where board edges sit higher than the middle. With continued exposure, a board can buckle, lifting completely off the subfloor, or crown, its middle higher than the edges because the upper face dries quicker than the underside. When a floor stays wet a long time, mold can also develop under the boards, on the subfloor, unnoticed until they are pulled.

Is a Wet Hardwood Floor Salvageable?

Saving a wet hardwood floor comes down to the subfloor's condition, the hardwood's type and finish, the length of time it stayed wet, the amount of water, and how soon professional drying starts. Floors exposed to clean water can often be saved when professional drying starts within 24 to 48 hours. What makes it work is slow, controlled drying, so moisture leaves the wood evenly and the boards do not split or crack.

Drying Wood Floors the Professional Way

Drying hardwood is not done the way ordinary structural drying is, and it calls for its own techniques. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Another option is to run a floor drying system under the subfloor, reaching it through the crawl space. Slow and even is the rule: rush it and the surface dries ahead of the core, leaving permanent splits, cracks, and cupping.

Signs the Floor Cannot Be Saved

Replacement is usually the answer if the subfloor is compromised or has mold on it, the boards have buckled or delaminated extensively, sewage, floodwater, or other contaminated water touched the wood, or the floor stayed wet longer than 48 to 72 hours.
